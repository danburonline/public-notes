Reappraisal is a cognitive emotion regulation strategy that involves **changing how one interprets a situation in order to alter its emotional impact.** Rather than suppressing an emotional response after it arises, reappraisal intervenes earlier by modifying the meaning assigned to an event before a full emotional reaction is generated.

## Gross Process Model

Reappraisal is classified as an **antecedent-focused** strategy within James Gross's process model of emotion regulation. It operates at the **cognitive change** stage — after attention has been directed toward a situation but before a behavioural or physiological response is triggered. This distinguishes it from **response-focused** strategies like [suppression](#reappraisal-vs-suppression), which modulate emotion only after it has already unfolded.

## Mechanisms

Reappraisal can take several forms:

- **Positive reframing**: Finding a beneficial or less threatening interpretation of the situation (e.g., viewing a job rejection as redirection toward a better opportunity).
- **Distancing**: Adopting a detached, third-person perspective on an emotionally charged event.
- **Reality challenge**: Examining whether the emotional interpretation is accurate or distorted (e.g., "What evidence supports this fear?").

## [Appraisal](appraisal.md) And Reappraisal

Reappraisal builds directly on the appraisal framework. Where [primary appraisal](appraisal.md) evaluates whether a situation is relevant, benign, or [stressful](appraisal.md), reappraisal deliberately revises that initial evaluation. This makes it a metacognitive correction to appraisal biases or automatic interpretations — for instance, reinterpreting a stressful primary appraisal as a challenge rather than a threat.

## Reappraisal vs. Suppression

| Strategy | Timing | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Reappraisal** | Antecedent-focused (early) | Reduces negative emotion experience and expression; preserves cognitive resources |
| **Suppression** | Response-focused (late) | Reduces expressive behaviour but not subjective experience; increases physiological arousal and cognitive load |

Studies consistently show that reappraisal is more effective and less cognitively costly than suppression for regulating negative emotions.

## Clinical Applications

Reappraisal is a core technique in [cognitive-behavioural therapy](../03_mental_health_in_the_community/cognitive-behavioural_therapy.md) (CBT), where patients are trained to identify and restructure maladaptive interpretations — often called **cognitive restructuring**. It is applied across anxiety disorders, depression, and [stress](../03_mental_health_in_the_community/stress.md)-related conditions, and its habitual use is associated with greater psychological well-being and lower [distress](../03_mental_health_in_the_community/distress.md).
