Anterograde and retrograde amnesia name the **direction of the loss**, not a spared complement. A person can have both. Neither name means the other direction is intact.

**Anterograde amnesia** is a failure to form new lasting memories after the onset. Memories from before may still be available. That preservation is a separate fact, not part of the definition. Being unable to recall events from after the onset is the same failure seen from the other side: those events were not stored. It is not a retrieval deficit for memories that were laid down.

**Retrograde amnesia** is a loss of memories formed before the onset. New learning may be intact, and often is not. The loss is usually graded. Recent memories go before remote ones. That gradient is a tendency, not a clean cut at the moment of injury.

The textbook pair — new memories gone and old ones kept, or the reverse — is a useful contrast. It is not two sealed disorders.
