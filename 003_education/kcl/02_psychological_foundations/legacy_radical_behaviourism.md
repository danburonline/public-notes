#core/appliedneuroscience

**Radical [behaviourism](behaviourism.md) is the philosophy of behaviour science associated with B.F. [Skinner](the_operant_chamber_skinner_box.md), holding that all behaviour — including private events such as thoughts and feelings — is shaped by environmental selection.**

## Core Tenets

- **Environment over internal states**: Behaviour is explained by the environment that selects and maintains it, not by mentalistic constructs ("thoughts", "feelings") invoked as causes.
- **Private events are behaviour**: Unlike methodological behaviourism, radical behaviourism does not deny inner events — it treats them as *behaviour* subject to the same reinforcement laws as public action, not as causal explanations.
- **Selection by consequences**: Skinner's functional account — [operant learning](operant_learning.md) shaped by [reinforcement](reinforcement_and_reinforcers.md) and its [schedules](reinforcement_schedules.md); behaviour persists, changes, or undergoes [extinction](extinction_learning.md) depending on consequences.

## Contrast

- **Vs. cognitive psychology**: cognitive approaches posit internal representations and processes; radical behaviourism explains behaviour through its history of environmental selection.
- **Vs. psychoanalysis**: psychoanalytic approaches posit unconscious drives; radical behaviourism rejects unobservable mental mechanisms in favour of functional analysis.

## Connection to the Module

Radical behaviourism is the philosophical endpoint of the [behaviourism](behaviourism.md) family — the most committed extension of the environmentalist programme. It grounds the module's learning-theory cluster: Skinner's operant framework ([the operant chamber](the_operant_chamber_skinner_box.md)) is the experimental realisation of its central thesis.
