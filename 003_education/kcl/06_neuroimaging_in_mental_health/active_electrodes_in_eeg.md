#core/appliedneuroscience

EEG electrodes detect the **voltage fluctuations generated by neuronal [dipoles](dipoles_in_eeg.md)** at the scalp surface. Because these signals are extremely weak (microvolts), the way they are handled between the electrode and the amplifier critically affects signal quality. There are two main electrode types: **active** and **passive**.

## Active Electrodes

- **Definition**: Active electrodes have built-in preamplifiers that boost the EEG signal at the site of measurement. This converts the high-impedance signal at the scalp into a low-impedance output before it travels along the cable, reducing the amount of electrical noise picked up en route to the main amplifier.
- **Benefits**: They enhance signal quality by improving the signal-to-noise ratio, making them less susceptible to interference from environmental sources, such as powerline (50/60 Hz) noise and cable movement artefacts. They also tolerate higher electrode–skin impedance, so less skin preparation is required and setup is faster.
- **Usage**: Often used in research settings, high-density arrays and mobile EEG studies to obtain clearer signals and more accurate readings — advantageous when recording [event-related potentials](event-related_potential.md), where noise must be minimised.

## Passive Electrodes

- **Definition**: Passive electrodes have no built-in amplification. The EEG signal is picked up by the electrode and transmitted unamplified via wires to the main amplifier, which is usually some distance away.
- **Challenges**: More susceptible to noise, including powerline (50 or 60 Hz) interference, because the signal travels a longer distance without amplification, making it vulnerable to external electric fields. They therefore require low electrode–skin impedance, typically achieved through skin preparation (abrasion) and conductive gel.
- **Usage**: Commonly used in clinical settings due to their simplicity and lower cost compared to active electrodes.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preamplifier at electrode | Yes | No |
| Noise susceptibility | Low | High |
| Skin preparation needed | Minimal | Abrasion + conductive gel |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Typical setting | Research, high-density, mobile EEG | Clinical routine |
