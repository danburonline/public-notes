Takagaki and Ohl use **degrees of freedom** to frame cortical complexity at the **substrate** and **phenomenological** levels, rather than calculate a formal state-space dimension.

## Complexity, not a single count

Their heart–brain comparison (§1, Table 1) contrasts:

- **Substrate:** numbers and types of excitable cells, spatial network organisation and connectivity.
- **Dynamics:** multiple [timescales](time_scales_of_the_brain.md), from spiking to development, and diverse oscillatory modes.
- **Phenomena:** the range of physiological functions and pathological disturbances.

These heterogeneous measures support a qualitative argument for simplification; they cannot be added into a universal complexity score.

## Experimental consequence

The authors advocate [experimental coarse-graining](coarse_graining.md): simplified preparations, observations or tasks that isolate tractable phenomena, complementing detailed recordings and simulations. Spiral waves studied in drug-treated cortical slices were subsequently observed in vivo, including epileptiform cortex (§2).

**Synthesis:** A pharmacologically simplified cortex is a modified system, not just a reduced description. Transferring findings to intact cortex needs empirical validation; [dynamical abstraction](../_general/dynamical_abstraction.md) imposes a separate requirement that representation changes preserve specified dynamics. The chapter does not construct such a preservation map.
