#fundamental/logic

**Multidisciplinarity, interdisciplinarity, and transdisciplinarity** differ in how far disciplines are integrated. They are ideal types, not a hierarchy: the right mode depends on the problem.

- **Multidisciplinary:** disciplines address a common problem separately; their perspectives are juxtaposed, not integrated.
- **Interdisciplinary:** disciplines integrate concepts, methods, or frameworks into a coordinated account that none could produce alone. The label alone does not guarantee integration.
- **Transdisciplinary:** a contested term. It can mean inquiry *between*, *across*, and *beyond* disciplines, aiming at a unity of knowledge, or problem-oriented co-production that includes non-academic stakeholders. These senses overlap but are not identical.

Juxtaposition is easily mistaken for synthesis. Linking claims is not yet interdisciplinary; synthesis requires a shared object or mechanism, compatible assumptions, and evidence that the linked claims actually support the connection. Integrative examples include [consciousness engineering](../../_general/consciousness_engineering.md), [biomimetic neuromorphics](../../../002_profession/eightsix/biomimetic_neuromorphics.md), and [conceptual blending](../how_to_build_a_brain/conceptual_blending.md).
