**Functionalism** is a family of philosophy-of-mind theories that individuate mental states primarily by their causal-functional roles — relations to sensory inputs, other mental states, and behaviour — rather than by identifying every mental kind with one specific material or neural kind ([Janet Levin, “Functionalism”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/functionalism/)). This contrast does not make functional roles non-physical: roles require physical realisers, and the systems that realise them may constrain which roles are possible.

## Major Varieties

These varieties overlap and are not mutually exclusive; computation and the thickness of a functional description vary independently:

- **Analytic functionalism**: individuates mental states by a priori or folk-psychological causal roles.
- **Psychofunctional or scientific functionalism**: identifies roles through empirical psychological theory and explanatory practice.
- **Machine-state or computational functionalism**: characterises mental states using abstract-machine, programme, or computational-architecture roles.

## How to Use This Map

The map is a user-made analytical map: a bespoke, non-quantitative interpretive classification of 98 entries, not a disciplinary taxonomy, set of consensus dimensions, or set of canonical locations. Positions are approximate. Filled points indicate that proponents explicitly brand a view as functionalism; open points indicate a view described through roles or functions without that label. The axes help compare emphases, but do not determine whether a theory is correct, consciousness is preserved, personal identity survives, substrate independence holds, or an approach is empirically feasible.

- **Computational commitment →**: how much computation the position builds into the functional object, from none (bare causal role) through generic and architectural computation to classical digital/TM and Turing-equivalence.
- **Thickness of the functional object →**: how much structure the functional description carries, from thin actual I/O and folk Ramsey roles, through scientific roles and machine table/programme/VM, up to fine causal topology, canonical Can(S), and physical-object/scale-inseparable descriptions.
- **Multiple realisability →** (colour): the map's judgement of how widely a view permits physical realisers to vary, from identity-like through population-relative and weak biological to Martian-silicon, medium independence, and Turing-equivalence. This is not a settled numeric property: theories differ over the relevant grain, realisation relation, and causal constraints ([John Bickle, “Multiple Realizability”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/multiple-realizability/)).

## Interpretive Neighbourhoods, Not Consensus Clusters

These groupings are interpretive neighbourhoods rather than claims that all entries are variants of functionalism or that the map resolves their disputes:

- **Left — role-centred and neighbouring**: analytic functionalism, realiser functionalism, teleosemantic positions, and semantic externalism. Teleosemantics can provide a causal or functional account of content without thereby being functionalism; externalist views can supplement or constrain role accounts.
- **Centre/right — computational and neurocomputational neighbourhood**: functionalist reduction, long- and short-arm functionalism, informational-state positions, embodied and extended functionalism, machine-state functionalism, computational functionalism, classical symbol systems, GOFAI-style CTM, and neurocomputational perspectives. Their computational commitments differ.
- **Top — mechanism-enriched, biologically constrained, and neighbouring**: physical functionalism, biological computationalism, and mechanism-enriched or strong mechanistic functionalism, alongside autopoietic/enactive positions whose relation to functionalism remains contested.

## Limits

- A functional role is not a uniform kind of description: analytic, psychological, computational, and mechanistic roles can differ in grain and explanatory purpose.
- Functionalism, computationalism, and multiple realisability are related but not automatically equivalent.
- The map does not resolve phenomenal consciousness, causal exclusion, the role of external content, or dependence on particular substrates.
