**Functionalism** is a family of positions in philosophy of mind that individuate mental states by their causal-functional roles — relations to inputs, outputs, and other mental states — rather than by material composition. The map situates 98 flavours (filled = self-branded functionalism; open = individuated by role or function without the brand) along three dimensions.

## Reading the Map

- **Computational commitment →**: how much computation the position builds into the functional object, from none (bare causal role) through generic and architectural computation to classical digital/TM and Turing-equivalence.
- **Thickness of the functional object →**: how much structure the functional description carries, from thin actual I/O and folk Ramsey roles, through scientific roles and machine table/program/VM, up to fine causal topology, canonical Can(S), and physical-object/scale-inseparable descriptions.
- **Multiple realisability →** (colour): how liberal the realisability is, from identity-like through pop-relative and weak biological to Martian-silicon, medium independence, and Turing-equivalence.

## Clusters

- **Left (no computational commitment)**: causal-role and teleosemantic traditions — analytic functionalism, realiser functionalism, teleosemantics, and semantic externalist positions.
- **Centre (generic/architectural computation)**: functionalist reduction, long- and short-arm functionalism, informational-state positions, embodied and extended functionalism, and neurocomputational perspectives.
- **Right (classical digital/TM)**: machine-state functionalism, computational functionalism, classical symbol systems, and GOFAI-style CTM.
- **Top (thick objects)**: physical functionalism, autopoiesis (the enactive root), biological computationalism, and mechanism-enriched or strong mechanistic functionalism.
