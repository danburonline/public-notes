A **lump-sum grant** fixes, in advance, the amount paid for each **work package** and pays it when the work package's activities are completed, instead of reimbursing actual costs. Horizon Europe uses this model, including for EIC Pathfinder projects ([European Commission guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/ls-funding-what-do-i-need-to-know_he_en.pdf)).

## Mechanism

- The budget is built bottom-up (person-months, costs) at proposal stage, then frozen as a **lump-sum share per beneficiary per work package**.
- Interim payments cover shares of work packages completed and accepted in the reporting period; partial completion is paid only at the end of the project ([how to manage lump-sum grants](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-manage-your-lump-sum-grants_en.pdf)).
- Completion means the activities were carried out, not that the science succeeded.
- Financial reporting is largely automated; audits look at technical proof of work rather than timesheets against costs.

## Practical implications

- **Effort and money must line up per work package.** A partner listed with effort in a work package where it has no lump-sum share is inconsistent with the model, so effort tables and budget shares have to be reconciled before signature.
- **Staffing is flexible within a share**: who performs the work matters less than whether it is done and documented.
- **Work-package design is the contract**: clear, period-specific tasks and verifiable deliverables make completion easy to demonstrate.

## Comparison

Unlike a [SAFE](simple_agreement_for_future_equity.md), which buys future equity, a lump-sum grant buys completed activities. Both reward clear milestones, but only equity carries ownership.
